## What the always-on work found

**The second look at the line fired every four hours against a two-hour
window.** `_near_start_snapshots` acts on a prediction only while its game is
between now and two hours out, and its only caller was `refresh`, on a
four-hourly trigger. Measured on the day's twelve baseball games, **three fell
in no firing's window at all** — 19:10Z, 23:30Z and 00:10Z — and would have been
looked at once, at prediction time, and never again.

That pass is the only writer of a near-start venue ladder, an at-the-line claim
and a closing price. So the gap is also the answer to a question `READINESS.md`
had left open this morning: why `at_the_line_claims` and `recommendations` both
read zero while `venue_quotes` read 1,172. A quarter of every slate was
unreachable by construction, and the criterion that decides all the others —
the closing line — was capped at zero permanently rather than slowly.

**The window was not widened.** Two hours is a declared meaning: late enough
that the day's news is priced, early enough not to race the first pitch.
Widening it to fit the scheduler would have changed what "near the start"
measures in order to make a number appear, which is the move `READINESS.md`
exists to refuse. The clock moved instead.

## Rulings taken in the operator's absence

1. **A new task rather than a wider window, and rather than a faster
   `refresh`.** Refreshing every thirty minutes would re-read five sports'
   seasons forty-eight times a day to catch a two-hour window; the near-start
   pass on its own clock costs a no-op row most of the time. `refresh` still
   calls the same pass, which is safe because the pass is idempotent — it
   selects only predictions with no near-start row yet.
2. **`Gridiron-Serve` registered, and the Startup shortcut removed.** The
   earlier "Access is denied" was not elevation: the installer's own comment
   records that a logon trigger with no `-User` is a machine-wide change and
   Windows refuses it. Scoped to this user it registers unelevated, and it
   carries restart settings a shortcut cannot. Leaving both would have raced
   two processes for the same port.
3. **The installer was edited, not just the machine.** A task registered by
   hand and absent from `tools/schedule_install.ps1` is a task the next
   install silently drops. Both new tasks are in its create list and its
   remove list, so a reinstall stays idempotent.
4. **The whole installer was not re-run.** It unregisters every Gridiron task
   and re-registers it, and `Gridiron-Recalibrate` is due to fire for the first
   time at 06:00 this morning. Re-basing that trigger to make an edit tidier
   would have delayed the first correction fit by a week.
5. **The top-ranked question is reported to the operator as a forecast without
   a price, not as a pick.** He asked for a pick; the record does not contain
   one yet, and the honest answer to "give me a pick right now" is the ranking
   plus the reason nothing has cleared the bar.

## What a test caught, within a minute

`test_every_task_is_installable_and_worded` holds four lists together —
`tasks.TASKS`, `scheduler.OS_TASK_NAMES`, `language.TASK_WORDS` and the
installer's own `$TaskNames` array — and failed on the one of the four that had
not been updated. It was written after the UFC passes were declared in one list
and no other, and it has now paid for itself twice.
