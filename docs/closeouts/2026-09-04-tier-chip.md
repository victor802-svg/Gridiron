## 2. What the measurement found

Three questions, because the chip has to answer all three to be carrying the
argument ruling 3 rests on — *"a reader is told what a claim is worth instead
of having weak claims hidden from them."*

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| **Does it appear?** | **Yes.** 379 of 379 cards. |
| **Does it vary?** | **Yes**, in 16 of 18 markets. |
| **Is it earned?** | **17 of 379. 4.5%.** |

## 3. The defect that only a measurement could find

**The behaviour was correct.** `tier_from_bucket` refuses to state a hit rate
below 20 settled and composes *"STRONG — 3 settled, not yet proven; 20 needed
before it earns a verdict"* instead. Nothing was lying.

**It was invisible.** On a grid card that sentence reached the reader only
through `chip.title` — **a hover tooltip.** No hover on a phone, and no hover
at a glance anywhere. The hero printed it as visible text.

> **The one card a reader is shown in full was honest, and the thirty behind it
> were not.** That is the worst available split, because it makes the page look
> like it discloses.

**Fixed on this project's own precedent, three rulings old.** The coin-flip
note went on the collapsed card face rather than one tap in, *"because a caveat
behind a tap is a caveat most readers never reach, and the reader taking the
percentage at face value is exactly the one it is written for."* **A tooltip is
worse than a tap.**

- `language.tier_chip_label(tier, proven)` composes **"STRONG · unproven"**
  until the band earns a verdict, and **"STRONG"** after. One word, no number,
  so the cards brief's R2 — one number and the word for what it is a number of
  — is untouched.
- It is composed **where the tier is built** (`calibration.tier_from_bucket`),
  so every consumer gets it and the browser prints what it is given.
- `.tier-unproven` stops an unearned STRONG from looking like an earned one: no
  fill, a dashed border. **A filled STRONG chip that has never been right about
  anything is the most persuasive thing on the page.**
- The full sentence still travels and the hero still prints it. The chip is
  small; a reader only needs to know which of the two they are looking at.

**Asserted on the phone, deliberately.** `title` is exactly the mechanism that
works where a desktop test would look and fails where a reader actually is, so
the browser test runs at 390px and checks the text, not the attribute.

### And the guard was satisfied by its own comment

The first version of `tier_chip_faults` scanned the whole `tierChip` body for
`chip_label`. **The paragraph inside that function explaining why it renders
`chip_label` satisfied the check**, so deleting the actual code left the guard
green — the planting caught only one of its two halves.

This is verbatim the failure `_caller_sources` documents a thousand lines
above it: *"a guard satisfied by a comment about the guard is worse than the
finding it silenced."* It now strips `//` comments before reading, and a test
hands it a function that only *talks* about `chip_label` to prove it.

## 4. A correction: "a rung question cannot reach 70%" was wrong

I wrote that in `config.GAME_MARKET_MIN_CLAIM`'s reasoning yesterday. **It
does.** On the live slates, hours later:

| | STRONG | of | median settled behind those chips |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CFB spread | **49** | 60 | 5 |
| CFB total | **31** | 57 | 1 |
| NFL spread | 2 | 29 | 0 |
| MLB spread | 3 | 15 | 0 |

What is confined to 45.8%–54.2% is the contribution **of the rung offset**. The
fitted factors add to it, and in college football they add a great deal.

**The correction makes the case against a floor stronger, not weaker.** Those
CFB chips have a median of **5** settled picks behind them on the spread and
**1** on the total — the sport's recorded over-confidence (spread base rate
0.371) arriving as confident claims nothing has tested. **A 70% floor would
have kept precisely those** and discarded the LEAN cards beside them, on no
evidence that the confident ones are better. That is the same shape as the
read-out's failure, in a second method.

> **A floor is a bet that confidence predicts accuracy. This project has now
> measured that relationship twice: absent once, reversed once.**

## 6. Rulings taken in your absence

1. **The defect was fixed, not only reported.** You asked for a measurement.
   The measurement found a caveat that a reader cannot see, and this project
   has a dated ruling on exactly that pattern. Leaving it after measuring it
   would have been the half-built item the contract warns about. **It is one
   word on a chip and is trivially reversible.**
2. **"unproven", not the whole sentence.** A chip is small, the sentence
   already travels and the hero already prints it, and the body carries the
   numbers. What the chip owes a reader is which of the two they are looking
   at.
3. **The unproven STRONG loses its fill.** Saying "unproven" in the same
   confident styling would have been half a fix.
4. **The floor ruling is untouched.** §4 strengthens it; nothing here reopens
   it.

## 7. What this does not settle

**Whether 4.5% is a problem or a phase.** Every one of those chips becomes
earned as the record fills — MLB's moneyline is already at 71 settled, and it
got there in weeks. The measurement is a snapshot of a project whose forward
record is young, and re-running `measure_tier_chip.py` in a month is the way to
tell a young record from a stalled one.

**Whether the chip is now enough.** It is honest, it varies, and it says which
of the two it is. Whether a reader working through a slate actually uses it is
a question about people, and this session measured the page rather than them.
