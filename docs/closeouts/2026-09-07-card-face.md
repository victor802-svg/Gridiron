## What the work found that the brief did not

**Two functions were defined twice in the renderer.** `renderToday`, byte for
byte identical, one directly after the other — half of it shipped as dead
weight. And `localTime`, 571 lines apart, **not identical**: one returns an
empty string for a date that will not parse and the other returns the raw
instant, which would have printed `2026-09-07T17:10:00Z` on a card. The second
of each wins at load, so the copy a reader is most likely to find and fix is
the dead one. `audit.duplicate_js_definitions` fails the gate on the shape now,
with a planting.

**The renderer was building two sentences of its own**, and the scan that
exists to stop that could not see either: the countdown's
`state_word + ' in ' + span`, and the worked example's caption glued from a
dash, a subject and a percentage. The rule only fires on a line that also
mentions one of a dozen hand-written field names, which is its declared
weakness. Both compositions moved to `language`; the field list gained the
names that had actually been glued.

**Static assets carried no cache directive**, so a browser was free to reuse
`app.js` without asking — and did. The new markup rendered with the old
renderer, which produces an empty panel and reports nothing anywhere. That is
the same class as the stale-bundle check the launcher already has, one layer
down. `/static` now says `no-cache`, which means "ask first", not "do not
store".

**A label nobody could read.** The price boxes drew their labels in
`--faintest` on the page ground: **3.39:1**, against WCAG AA's 4.5 for text
that size. `tools/contrast.py` passed because the pair was not in its list.
Three new pairs are in it now.

**The page disagreed with itself about the count.** On the first priced slate,
the strip said "nothing clears the bar · 1 more below your 1.5x floor" while
the fold said "1 more clear the bar". The pick had cleared both conditions and
is on the record; only the operator's display floor kept it out of the cards.
The count is of picks that clear the bar, folded ones included, or the floor
starts looking like a rule about bets.

**An edge with no side.** A card read "Carolina covers -2.5", model 52¢, venue
60¢, edge **+6.0¢** — and the edge is on the *other* side of that question. A
question, a plus sign and a green number all say "back this", and all three
were wrong together. This is the defect this project has had most often;
`check_side_named_everywhere` exists because of it. The edge label now says
"on the other side" when it is, and the side travels with the number whether or
not the pick clears the bar.

**And the record cannot produce a claim at all yet.** Measured while trying to
render a priced card: **no prediction in the record carries the frozen
distribution an at-the-line claim requires** — zero of them. Three reasons and
only the third is fixable soon:

- baseball has no measured margin spread by design, because its margins are
  counts, and `questions.blind_distribution` returns nothing rather than
  guessing;
- the fights have no margin at all;
- football does have one, and has not been forecast since the distribution
  began to be written on 6 September. Its slate is weekly and the next run is
  **9 September**.

**This corrects what was said earlier today.** The near-start cadence fix was
reported as the reason claims and recommendations were empty, with the first
claims "due at 15:05Z". The cadence gap was real and is fixed; it was not the
whole story, and no claim could have been written this afternoon whatever the
clock said. The first ones are due on the 9th, from football.

## Rulings taken in the operator's absence

1. **The size figure asks for the unit rather than inventing it.** The brief
   says the unit comes from the declared bankroll constant; that constant says
   explicitly that nothing in this codebase is an amount. A dollar figure
   invented here would be the app telling the operator what his money is.
2. **"Bet $5" is written "$5".** `audit.ADVICE_WORDS` has held "bet" since
   before LAW 5 was amended, and `language.recommendation_line` has always said
   "one flat unit". The dollar figure is the substance the brief asked for; the
   imperative verb adds only urgency, which this brief bans by name elsewhere.
3. **The pressure words scan the words this project writes, not the model's.**
   Put in `plain_words_violations` as the brief asked, the list failed two
   stored rows that say "boost" — a boost from playing at home. `ADVICE_WORDS`
   and `TIPSTER_WORDS` already draw exactly this line.
4. **Today's panel did not move above the hero.** Two control rows are allowed
   there and its cards carry buttons. The strip carries none, so the strip
   went instead and the law is untouched.
5. **`.up` and `.down` carry the value colours.** The colour law's own selector
   rule admits them, so the edge is green and red without a new token and
   without a second job for either.
