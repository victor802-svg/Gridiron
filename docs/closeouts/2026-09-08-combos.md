## The law this brief reverses

> **SINGLES ONLY.** The engine never recommends a parlay and refuses to price
> one: each leg pays the spread, so an N-leg parlay multiplies the cost of
> being right by roughly N, and correlated legs price worse than they look.

It is a paragraph of LAW 5, it has a function that raises and a planting that
proves the function fires, and the brief never names it.

**THE AMENDMENT PROCEDURE EXISTS AND IS THE OPERATOR'S TO USE.** LAW 5 was
amended once already, on 2026-09-07: the ruling named the law, quoted the text
it replaced in full, gave the reason, and dated it. The replaced text still
stands in `CLAUDE.md` beneath the new one, because — this project's own words
— *a law that quietly vanished is a law nobody can audit*. One sentence from
the operator invokes that procedure. Reversing SINGLES ONLY as a side effect
of a feature brief is the one way it should not happen.

## What was measured

**1. The venue publishes combos, and it assembles them itself.** Six sports
combo series exist, and every one of them is the venue's own package:

| series | what it is |
|---|---|
| `KXNBAPREPACK2ML` | 2-moneyline basketball combo, prepackaged |
| `KXNBAPREPACK3ML` | 3-moneyline basketball combo, prepackaged |
| `KXNCAAMB2ML` | 2-moneyline college basketball combo |
| `KXNCAAMBSGP` | college basketball **same game parlay** |
| `KXNFLCOMBO` | football combo |
| `KXMLBAWARDCOMBO` | baseball **awards**, not games |

**This is the finding that decides C2.** The brief has the app choose two legs
by edge and then read the venue's price for that pair. That pair is not a
product: the venue sells the packages it chose. Either the app assembles and
the price must be computed — which C2 forbids in its own words — or the price
is read and the venue assembled, which reverses C1. Against this venue they
cannot both hold.

**2. There is no baseball game combo at all.** The only baseball entry is an
award combo. Baseball is the operator's daily sport and the only one with
venue quotes in this record; the two basketball prepacks and the football one
answered **0 open events** when asked on 2026-09-08.

**3. The legs exist, on the current slate.** Four picks clear the bar, from
four different games, all baseball — so the group would have material if the
rest held.

**4. The fee is worse than the brief says for three legs, and fair for two.**
Measured with the declared formula:

| stake | fee per dollar staked |
|---|---|
| a single at 60¢ | 3.33% |
| two 60¢ legs, combined at 36¢ | 5.56%, which is 1.67× |
| three 60¢ legs, combined at 21.6¢ | 9.26%, which is 2.8× |

The brief's heading sentence — "about twice the singles' per dollar" — is
right for two legs and understates three.

**5. Nothing on the record has earned any of this.** No recommendation has
ever cleared its market's gate: every size ever written is the flat unit, 8
rows, all flat. The closing line, which is the criterion that decides whether
the model beats a price at all, stands at zero observations. A product whose
cost per dollar is between 1.7 and 2.8 times the singles' would be launched on
top of an edge that has never been measured once.

## Rulings taken in the operator's absence

1. **The brief was saved and not executed.** The standing agreement is to save
   each brief before executing it; a brief that arrived is part of the record
   whether or not it runs.
2. **The conflict was reported rather than resolved.** The standing agreement
   resolves forks by law first, and the law here is not ambiguous about what
   it forbids — only about whether this particular paragraph is amendable. The
   four bulleted prohibitions in LAW 5 say "NOT amendable by a later session";
   SINGLES ONLY is a separate paragraph and carries no such line, so it is
   amendable — by the operator, by name, not by inference from a feature
   brief.
3. **The measurements were done anyway.** They are the part of the brief that
   is useful whatever he rules, and two of them change what a lawful version
   of this brief would even look like.
