## 2. The question

Combined points, asked at the declared ladder rung nearest the expected total.

**The expectation** is each club's own scoring form against the other's
defence: `(home_for + away_against)/2 + (away_for + home_against)/2`. Blind by
construction — every input is points scored and allowed in our own stored
results.

**The band is 185–278**, the 1st and 99th percentiles of 4,920 stored games
(mean 229.2, SD 19.9, full range 152–351). An expectation outside it is
**refused, not clamped**: the game gets no total question and still gets its
other two.

**Every rung is a half-point, so nothing can push.** A pushed question has no
answer to score, which would be a void on the market whose whole appeal is that
it always resolves. Planted.

**A total is a sum, not a difference**, and every factor reflects it. The
spread and moneyline ask which club is better and take differences; a total
asks how much basketball happens and takes sums. `nba_availability_sum` is
declared beside `nba_availability_index` for exactly the reason
`ufc_finish_rate_sum` sits beside `ufc_finish_rate_diff`: **two clubs each
missing three starters have a difference of zero and a combined score well
below normal.** `nba_home_court` is deliberately absent — home advantage adds
points to one side and removes them from the other, so its effect on a combined
score is close to nothing.

## 4. THE FINDING: this market measures almost nothing, and here is why

### 4.1 The first build measured literally nothing

Asked at the expectation itself, rounded to the nearest half:

```
Brier 0.2500   always-base-rate 0.2506   edge +0.0006
hit rate 50.9%
every one of 2,460 test rows in the 50-60% bucket
```

**That is not a weak model. It is a question with no content.** If the rung
*is* the expectation then P(over) is one half by construction, and the only
thing a coefficient can find is the rounding.

### 4.2 A quantised ladder put some of it back

Five-point steps across the band — the same shape the spread ladder has, and
for the same reason. A game expected to total 233 is asked at 230.5, and the
three points of difference are expressed as a **probability** rather than
absorbed by the **question**.

```
Brier 0.2495   always-base-rate 0.2505   edge +0.0010
hit rate 53.6%   (was 50.9%)
50-60%   n=2276  said 54.5%  hit 53.3%  gap -1.2
60-70%   n= 184  said 61.0%  hit 57.1%  gap -4.0
weighted |gap| 1.38 points over 2,460 rows
```

The asked-distance coefficient **doubled**, −0.093 → −0.183, which is the
ladder working. The edge went from +0.0006 to +0.0010, which is still nothing.

### 4.3 Why, and what it means for the roster

**A total asked at one's own rung is a coin flip by design.** That is not a
defect in this model — it is what a well-built totals forecast looks like when
it also gets to choose the question. Books set totals to be coin flips; we set
ours the same way, because LAW 1 forbids us from seeing theirs.

**The value in a totals market is disagreeing with someone else's number**, and
under LAW 1 we cannot have one before we predict. So the market is honest,
perfectly balanced, always resolvable — and structurally incapable of much
edge.

**THIS APPLIES TO NFL TOTAL, roster #19**, which the session's STEP 4 reaches
next. It is the same construction and should be expected to behave the same
way. That is worth knowing before it is built rather than after.

### 4.4 It was still shipped, and the reason

It is not a market the decline criterion covers: **not one-sided** (48.5% base
rate is the best balance on the roster), **not thin** (100% resolution), **not
low volume** (15 a slate). It is a legitimate, well-calibrated record that will
simply never claim much. **The gate will hold it honest**: 100 settled
predictions before any edge figure renders, and on this evidence none will
clear.

**One consequence for the operator to rule on.** Game markets carry no
confidence floor — argued in the moneyline's close-out, and right there — so
every NBA night will now produce ~15 total cards at 50–55%. That is fifteen
cards a night that say very little. Whether a floor should exist for a market
that *structurally* cannot clear it is a decision, not a defect, and it is
yours.

## 5. Void rules, written before the first prediction

- **No score on a final game** → VOID.
- **A push** → impossible by construction; every rung is a half. Planted.
- **An expectation outside 185–278** → no question asked at all. Not a void:
  the question is never put, which is a different fact and is recorded as one.
- **A game not final** → not resolvable yet, never voided.

## 6. Checklist item 4

The total's own consistency check is the **push impossibility** above, planted
and verified across the whole ladder and the chooser.

**The moneyline↔spread cross-check does not extend to the total**, and that is
stated rather than skipped: a combined score and a margin are independent
quantities — a 240-point game can be a one-point win or a forty-point rout — so
there is no logical relation to assert between them. Inventing one would be the
opposite of what item 4 is for.
