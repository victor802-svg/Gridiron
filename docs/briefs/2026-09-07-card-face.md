Redesign the Today screen. The operator's rulings, taken 2026-09-07: he
browses and reads each pick rather than scanning a table; he wants a layout
that reads like a sportsbook, because he already knows how to read one; and
his eye should land on the edge after fees before anything else.

**THIS BRIEF TOUCHES THE SCREEN AND NOTHING BENEATH IT.** No model, ranker,
correction, snapshot or table changes. It is the one kind of work permitted
during the observation window because it cannot contaminate the record.

## What "sportsbook-familiar" means here, and what it does not

A sportsbook page is two things in one skin. The **grammar** — an event
header, a market line, price chips side by side, a running list of what you
took — is familiar because it is good information design for exactly this
content. The **pressure** — countdown timers, odds that flash on movement,
"hot" / "boost" / "trending" / "popular" badges, a parlay builder, streak
counters — exists to make wagering feel urgent, and is what makes a book
money. This brief takes the grammar and bans the pressure by name.

`audit`'s plain-words scan gains: `boost`, `hot`, `trending`, `popular`,
`streak`, `parlay`, `combo`, `same game`. `tools/guards/plant.py` puts one
in a card label and proves the scan fires. No element on the page may
animate on a price change; a planting adds a CSS transition to the price
chip and a test catches it. No countdown to kickoff is rendered anywhere —
kickoff is a time, not a timer.

## What the page must diagnose about itself before the redesign

Recorded in the close-out as findings, each with the fix that closed it:

1. The top group's rows showed **no number at all** while the cards beneath
   them showed the probability in 60-point type — the most important rows on
   the page carried the least information.
2. "No venue price to compare against yet" was appended to every row as a
   sentence, five times, as though each row discovered it independently.
3. Every card wore the same `STRONG · unproven` chip. A label identical on
   every item carries no information; it is a group heading wearing a badge.
4. The largest element on every card was the raw probability — the one
   number the record measures as least trustworthy — and the edge, the
   number that decides whether anything is a bet, had no visual home.
5. Nothing at the top of the page stated the state of the day. A reader had
   to read all of it to learn the answer was "nothing yet".

## PHASE F1 — The day, stated once

A status strip at the top of Today, one line, plain words, present every
day. The date and the slate ("Sunday 7 September · NFL week 1"), how many
picks clear the bar, how many are watched, when venue prices are expected if
none exist yet, and the fee line ("Kalshi's fee on a 50¢ contract today:
1.75¢"). Nothing below it needs to be read to know what kind of day it is.

## PHASE F2 — The card, rebuilt around the edge

One card design for both groups; the group heading, not the card, says
which group it belongs to.

**Header** — the sportsbook event line: matchup, kickoff time in the
operator's zone, sport. Not a countdown.

**Market line** — the question in the plain words the scan already enforces:
"Ja'Marr Chase under 9.5 receptions".

**The price row — three chips, side by side, the edge chip largest and the
only one with colour:**

| chip | reads | size |
|---|---|---|
| Model | fair value as a price: `88¢` | small, quiet |
| Venue | the recorded price and what it pays: `84¢ · pays 1.2x`, or `no price yet` | small, quiet |
| **Edge** | after fees, signed: `+2¢` / `−3¢` | **large; green above zero, red below, grey when no price** |

When no venue price exists, the Venue chip reads `no price yet` in those
words and the Edge chip reads `—`. **The sentence is never repeated on the
row**; the status strip already said it once for the whole slate.

**Size, on cards that clear the bar only** — a dollar figure, not a word:
"Bet $5 · flat unit, edge unproven" below the gate, or the quarter-Kelly
figure once the market is past its gate. The unit comes from the declared
bankroll constant (THE_PRICED D3) and nothing else. Watching cards carry no
size, because a size on a pick that does not clear the bar is a
recommendation the app is not making.

**Meta row** — gate status in words ("52 settled · 48 more before a
verdict"), and the tier chip **only when it differs from the group's
default**; when every card in a group would wear the same chip, the group
heading carries it instead and the cards carry none.

**The taken control** — "I took this", styled the way a book styles its
add-to-list control so the hand knows where it is. It records which pick and
when, and nothing else, exactly as GRIDIRON_TODAY built it.

**Reasoning** — the operator reads each pick, so the card expands on click
to the reasoning pass's prose and the factor contributions that already
exist on the Picks cards. Collapsed by default so the page still scans.

## PHASE F2b — Worth taking: return on stake, and the operator's floor

Ruled by the operator 2026-09-07: picks that pay too little are not worth
the click. Two mechanisms, kept separate on purpose.

**The bar gains a second condition.** A pick clears the bar only if its edge
after fees is positive AND its **return on stake** — edge divided by the
venue's price — meets a declared minimum, `MIN_RETURN_ON_STAKE`, proposed at
5%, dated in config with the rationale: a 2¢ edge on an 89¢ contract is real
and is also 2% on the money, and at the operator's unit that is pennies. This
is a property of the bet, so it belongs in the bar.

**A payout floor is a display preference, not a rule about bets.** Settings
gains "Minimum payout", default 1.5x, set by the operator and dated when
changed. Picks that clear the bar but pay below the floor are not shown as
cards; they fold into one line under the group — "3 more clear the bar
below your 1.5x floor" — and expand on click. The engine records every pick
that clears the bar regardless of the floor, so CLV on the 21st is measured
on all of them and, separately, on the ones the floor let through. Both
figures render with their N. A floor that hid picks from the record would be
the operator's taste rewriting the measurement.

The status strip counts both: "2 clear the bar · 1 below your floor".

## PHASE F3 — Taken today

The list of tapped picks moves from anonymous rows at the top into a
labelled panel, "Taken today", in the position a book puts its running
list: right rail on desktop, bottom sheet on the phone. Each entry shows the
pick, the edge **at the moment it was tapped**, and the time. No stake, no
payout, no total — it is a selection record, not a slip, and the word "slip"
stays out of the interface. Removing a tap is a second append-only row, not
a delete (LAW 3's logic applied to the operator's own record).

## PHASE F4 — Both widths

Desktop is primary. The phone layout at 390px must still hold, per the
existing phone pass: cards stack to one column, the three chips stay on one
line, the right rail becomes a bottom sheet, tap targets keep their 44px
floor. The existing overlap, overflow and contrast tools run on every
route at both widths.

## How this brief is read

**The five diagnosed findings are the operator's, and they are checked
before they are fixed.** A finding recorded in a close-out as closed, having
never been confirmed to exist, is worse than no finding: it teaches the next
reader that the page was measured when it was described. Each of the five is
verified against the rendered page first, and any that does not hold is
written up as NOT FOUND with what was there instead.

**F2b changes what clears the bar, which is not screen work.** The brief
opens by saying nothing beneath the screen changes, and then adds a second
condition to the bar. That is a real change to a recommendation's meaning,
so it is treated as such: a dated constant with its rationale, a law-level
comment on why the floor is a display preference and the return minimum is
not, and no silent re-scoring of anything already recorded. Nothing in the
record is rewritten; the condition applies to what gets written next.

**The right rail is a layout, and `nav_faults` rules the nav at four pages.**
Taken today is a panel on the Picks route, not a fifth page, for the same
reason Today itself is.
