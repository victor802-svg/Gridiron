## How this brief is read

Two things, one of them a ruling and one of them work.

- **"CFB totals stay" settles the first open question from the previous
  close-out's §7.** A market whose expectation explains 0.93% of the variance
  is still asked, shown with its caveat. No code changes — CFB's total is
  already declared, already flagged, and already guarded against withdrawal.
  What it needs is the ruling recorded so the next session does not reopen it.
- **"The tier chip needs measuring against a real slate" is the work**, and it
  comes from the second open question: ruling 3 removed nothing, but it leaned
  on the tier chip to do the job a floor would have done — *"a reader is told
  what a claim is worth instead of having weak claims hidden from them"*. That
  was asserted, never measured.

**What measuring it means.** The chip earns its place only if, on a slate a
reader actually works through, it (a) appears, (b) VARIES, and (c) carries an
earned record or says plainly that it has none. A chip that reads the same on
every card in a market conveys nothing, and the argument for having no floor
would rest on something that is not doing any work.

**This measures and reports. It does not set a floor** — ruling 3 stands, and
overturning it would be the operator's, not mine.
