PHASE P1 — the record shows how close it is (commit "prog 1: gates")
=====================================================================

Every gate in the app becomes visible progress, in one component:
- TIER ROWS: beneath each tier's verdict, a thin progress line to
  the 20-settled verdict gate ("14 of 20"), and once past it, to
  the 100 edge gate. The line is chrome on hairline — no green (a
  filling bar is not a win).
- A "CLOSEST VERDICT" line at the top of the Record tab, per sport:
  "Closest to a verdict: LEAN — 6 more settled, about 2 slates at
  the current pace." The pace estimate is computed from the last
  14 days of settled counts and LABELLED an estimate; below 7 days
  of history it says "pace unknown" rather than guessing.
- The other counters get the same treatment where they already
  exist as text: corrections ("fitted at 50, applied ~200: 31 of
  50"), drift pairs ("0 of 50"), the rung window ("read on Sep 14 —
  12 days"). One component, one implementation, plain words, N on
  every line.
- Plantings: a progress line rendering a percentage in place of a
  count; a gate line without its N; green used on a progress bar.

PHASE P2 — the season as a shape (commit "prog 2: calendar")
=====================================================================

History, per active sport (Law 6), gains a RESULTS CALENDAR above
the table:
- One square per day with a settled result; inside it, wins–losses
  as small mono ("4–3"). Tint by the day's balance: green-tinted
  when more right than wrong, red-tinted when fewer, neutral when
  even or single-result. Semantic colours on results only — the
  colour law allows exactly this use.
- Voids shown as a faint dot count, never as wins or losses.
- Click a day: the table below filters to that day; the square
  shows a selected ring; a "clear" control restores the season.
- Weeks as rows, months labelled, the current day outlined. Empty
  days render as hairline squares — the shape of the season
  includes the quiet days.
- If the operator has calls (GRIDIRON_12), a toggle switches the
  calendar between "model" and "you (informed)" — never a combined
  view.
- The table keeps its sentence rows, chips and filters; the
  calendar is an additional lens, not a replacement.
- Plantings: a calendar merging sports; a void counted as a loss; a
  square tinted by anything other than the day's result balance.

PHASE P3 — settings, with a fence (commit "prog 3: settings")
=====================================================================

THE BOUNDARY, stated first: two kinds of knobs live in this project.
OPERATIONAL knobs (when tasks run, quiet hours, notification
toggles, the ntfy topic) are the operator's to change in-app.
MODEL AND LAW constants (the props floor, ladders, gates,
thresholds, factor sets, margin SDs) are DATED RULINGS — they are
SHOWN on the settings page read-only, each with its date and a
one-line note: "changing this is a ruling; edit config with a dated
note." A planting that makes any of them editable from the page
fails by name.

- Operational settings move OUT of .env into an append-only
  settings table (history of every change: when, old, new) with a
  current view. .env keeps secrets only (tokens, the ntfy topic
  value); the settings page shows the topic MASKED with a "rotate"
  action that calls the existing make_token path.
- The page: one section per subsystem in plain words — "When it
  runs" (per-sport predict times, resolve cadence, refresh offset),
  "Quiet hours", "Notifications" (results / failures toggles,
  channels), "Access" (rotate token, rotate ntfy topic, connected
  devices count), and the read-only "Rulings in force" section.
- Changing a schedule time re-registers the OS task through the
  existing installer script, then re-reads the OS scheduler and
  shows what it actually holds — the page never claims a change it
  did not verify. Failure to re-register is shown in words.
- Every change is authenticated, CSRF-protected (the session cookie
  is SameSite=Strict already; add a per-form token anyway), and
  logged to the settings history, which the page shows as "recent
  changes".
- Plantings: an unauthenticated settings write; a model constant
  editable from the page; a schedule change claimed without the OS
  re-read; a settings row updated in place instead of appended.
